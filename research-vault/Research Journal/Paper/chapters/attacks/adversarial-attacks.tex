\section{Adversarial Attacks}

The current state-of-the-art method for adversarial attacks against neural networks
is the projected gradient descent (PGD) attack \textsubscript{\cite{madry2019deeplearningmodelsresistant}},
an iterative method that seeks to find the optimal perturbation maximizing
the loss function of the neural network. The simulation and defense of a neural network
using the PGD method will be left for future analysis. In this paper, we examine
an alternative method of attack simulation using additive Gaussian noise
perturbation\textsubscript{\cite{Hajari2023}} on the error channel, $e(k)$.

\subsection{Simulation of Attacked CSTR System}

The trajectories of state variables $x_1(k)$ and $x_2(k)$ under adversarial
perturbation are presented in \autoref{fig:attacked-output-comparison}.
\autoref{fig:attacked-matlab-x1} and \autoref{fig:attacked-matlab-x2} show
the MATLAB reference implementation results for conversion $x_1$ and temperature $x_2$,
respectively. \autoref{fig:attacked-cpp-x1} and \autoref{fig:attacked-cpp-x2}
show the corresponding C++ port results. Both implementations exhibit degraded
tracking performance once the stochastic perturbation ($\sigma = 2$) is introduced,
confirming that the adversarial attack model produces consistent effects across
platforms.
