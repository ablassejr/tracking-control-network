The tracking control problem is one of the most fundamental practical problems
in control theory, where the goal is to design a controller that allows a system
to follow a desired trajectory or reference signal over time. In recent years,
neural networks have been increasingly used in control systems due to their ability
to approximate complex nonlinear functions and adapt to changing conditions. However,
the use of neural networks in control systems also introduces new vulnerabilities,
particularly to adversarial attacks. Small, practically undetectable perturbations
in the input of a neural network can lead to outsized adverse effects on the output,
which can be particularly problematic in control systems where safety and reliability are paramount.
\par
A tracking control problem can be formulated as designing such a system whose output
approximately repeats a given time-varying reference trajectory~\cite{tymoshchuk2024tracking}.
The defense of a tracking control neural network against adversarial perturbations
requires that, under the influence of such perturbations, tracking performance
remains acceptable within certain conditions. These conditions vary and are dependent
upon the designed control law's inherent robustness, the magnitude and nature of
the perturbation, and the performance metrics required of the system for safe operation.
The channel of attack chosen for this analysis is the error dynamics of the tracking
control system described in \eqref{eq:error_dynamics}~\cite{tymoshchuk2024tracking}.
\par
A Continuous Stirred-Tank Reactor (CSTR) system is chosen as a common close analog
to compare and confirm the results of the analysis. The CSTR is a widely studied
system in control theory, making it suitable for comparison with competitive analogs.
\par
A tracking control neural network (NN) for the known affine in the inputs, discrete-time
nonlinear system was presented in \cite{tymoshchuk2024tracking}. The paper describes
a method of tracking control that is of moderate complexity and requires no training.
This method uses a signum activation function of the tracking error, as well as a diagonal matrix of the
learning rate parameters to drive the system state towards the reference trajectory
in finite time. The method is inherently robust to bounded uncertainties and disturbances.
A block diagram of the controlled nonlinear system was presented, and the operation of the
controller simulated using a virtual CSTR analog designed in MATLAB.
\par
This paper builds upon the method presented in \cite{tymoshchuk2024tracking} by designing
an active defense against adversarial perturbations exceeding the inherent robustness
stability threshold on the tracking control NN. A block diagram of the defended
system is presented, and the operation of the defended controller is simulated
using a virtual CSTR analog. The results and performance of the nominal simulations are
compared between C++ and MATLAB implementations to confirm numerical consistency,
and reasonable complexity across platforms.
\section{A Discrete Time Tracking Control Neural Network}
Discrete-time neural networks, when compared to their continuous-time counterparts,
offer several advantages in control applications. They are often easier to implement
in digital hardware, can be more computationally efficient, and can be designed to
operate at specific sampling rates. Another distinct advantage is their relative
resilience to perturbations~\cite{cichocki1993neural}. They can
also be efficiently implemented and miniaturized with digital hardware.
