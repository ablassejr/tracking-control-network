\subsection{Simulation of Defended System}

The rounding defense~\eqref{eq:rounding_defense} is applied to the CSTR
system from \eqref{eq:cstr_dynamics}. Additive Gaussian noise with
$\sigma = 0.3$ is injected into the output of the signal function. Because
$\sigma < \tfrac{1}{2}$, the condition $|\epsilon(k)| < \tfrac{1}{2}$ holds
with high probability ($\approx 90\%$ of time steps), and the rounding
operation recovers the correct signal function value at those steps.

Fig.~\ref{fig:defended-state-tracking} presents the defended state trajectories.
Because the rounding correctly recovers $\operatorname{sgn}(e(k))$ for the
vast majority of time steps, the closed-loop dynamics closely approximate
the unperturbed system~\eqref{eq:closed_loop}, and the state variables
$x_1(t)$ and $x_2(t)$ recover near-unperturbed tracking performance.
