\subsection{Simulation of Defended System}

The rounding defense~\eqref{eq:rounding_defense} is applied to the CSTR
system from \eqref{eq:cstr_dynamics}. Additive Gaussian noise with
$\sigma = 0.5$ is injected into the output of the signal function, the same
perturbation magnitude as in the attacked system. Because
$\sigma = \tfrac{1}{2}$, the sufficient condition
$|\epsilon(k)| < \tfrac{1}{2}$ holds at approximately $68\%$ of time steps.
During active tracking where
$\operatorname{sgn}(e(k)) \in \{-1, +1\}$, the rounding correctly recovers
the signal function value whenever the perturbation does not cross the
nearest decision boundary, yielding correct recovery at approximately
$84\%$ of time steps.

Fig.~\ref{fig:composite-attack-defense} presents the defended state
trajectory $x_2(t)$ overlaid with the attacked trajectory for direct
comparison (top panel), along with tracking error $e_2(t)$ for both
systems (bottom panel). The defended trace exhibits substantially
improved tracking relative to the undefended attacked system,
confirming that the rounding defense provides effective mitigation
at the theoretical recovery threshold.
