A neural network (NN) tracking control for discrete-time nonlinear systems
with known internal dynamics is designed. The adversarial attack on the
tracking control NN is modeled as additive stochastic noise injected into
the output of the signal function of the controller. The adversarially perturbed system
and the corresponding closed-loop tracking dynamics are derived, and the
resulting degradation in tracking performance is demonstrated. The defense
is based on rounding the noisy output of the signal function. When the
perturbation magnitude is less than one half, the rounding recovers the
correct signal function value, and the defended closed-loop dynamics reduce
to the unperturbed system. The nominal C++ simulation is validated against
the MATLAB reference implementation with agreement to machine
precision, and the defended C++ response is compared against the nominal
reference implied by the rounding-recovery condition
(Appendix~\ref{sec:cpp-implementation}).
