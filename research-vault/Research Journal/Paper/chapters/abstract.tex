A neural network (NN) tracking control for discrete-time nonlinear systems
with known internal dynamics is designed. The adversarial attack on the
tracking control NN is modeled as additive stochastic noise injected into
the signal function of the controller. The adversarially perturbed system
and the corresponding closed-loop tracking dynamics are derived, and the
resulting degradation in tracking performance is demonstrated. The defense
is based on rounding the noisy output of the signal function: if the
absolute value of the noisy input is less than one half, the output is
rounded to zero; otherwise it is rounded to plus or minus one according
to standard rounding rules. When this condition is satisfied, the defended
system recovers the exact behavior of the unperturbed system. Simulations
confirm that the defended controller recovers unperturbed tracking
performance under attack. A C++ port of the simulation is validated against
the MATLAB reference implementation, achieving element-wise agreement to
machine precision (Appendix~\ref{sec:cpp-implementation}).
